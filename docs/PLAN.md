# Cosmo Connect — Product Plan

Build phase-wise only when explicitly instructed.

---

## Problem Statement (0→1)

Build a community of cosmetology professionals and salons.

### Flywheel

1. Salon hiring demand
2. Identify missing skills
3. Create targeted training via academies
4. Professionals earn verified skills
5. Salon owners hire with confidence
6. More salons post jobs
7. More hiring data
8. Better understanding of future skill demand

## Solution

Build the LinkedIn for cosmetology, where real hiring demand determines what professionals learn, verified skills build trusted careers, and salons, educators, and beauty brands all participate in the same ecosystem.

---

## Phase 1 — Build the Community

Collect jobs from:

- Indeed
- Craigslist
- Facebook Groups
- Instagram
- Reddit
- Local salons
- Academy bulletin boards
- Independent salon websites

Instead of simply displaying jobs, analyze them.

**Example:** From 500 jobs → 120 balayage, 80 keratin, 65 extensions, 40 bridal makeup, 20 nail art. Now you know exactly what the market needs. No guessing.

**Plan:** Aggregate every cosmetology job listing in LA, SF, San Diego, Sacramento, and Vegas. Doesn't need to be pretty or fully automated — a clean, searchable board with 300–500 live listings is the magnet. Instagram and Facebook groups matter more than Indeed in this industry; stylists live there.

---

## Phase 2 — Use Jobs as Market Research

Acquire aspiring professionals first. They need help the most: jobs, education, mentors, recognition. Easier CAC than starting with salon owners.

**Offer them:**

- Free profile
- Job alerts
- Career guidance
- Free webinars
- Skill badges
- Resume builder
- Portfolio
- Community discussions

Goal isn't revenue — goal is users.

**Plan:** Attract professionals with free job alerts by skill and city. Skill-demand reports ("Top 10 skills LA salons are hiring for this month") shared into stylist Instagram/Facebook communities. Cheap to produce from own data; highly shareable.

---

## Phase 3 — Turn Demand into Education

If 120 jobs ask for balayage → partner with academies, master stylists, beauty educators → create "Professional Balayage Certification". Education driven by real hiring demand.

**Plan:**

- Let job data pick the first 3 classes. Don't guess curriculum. From aggregated CA + Vegas listings, count skill mentions. Usual suspects: balayage/color correction, hair extensions (hand-tied/tape-in), lash and brow, barbering fades, bridal styling — but let data rank them.
- Publish openly: "We analyzed 400 salon job posts in California and Vegas — here are the 5 skills salons want most." Signup magnet + proves demand-led thesis.
- Don't build courses. Partner with 2–3 independent educators per skill (they have audiences, need distribution/credentialing). Offer students + badge system; they bring content + followers.
- Approach 1–2 academies in LA and Vegas for co-branded advanced workshops.
- Format: live online masterclass + hands-on assessment day in-city (hair skills can't be fully learned on video).

---

## Phase 4 — Verify Skills

Verify skills through:

- Practical assessments
- Before/after portfolio
- Instructor approval
- Employer feedback
- Peer endorsements
- Client ratings

Salons trust the badge — more valuable than watching a video.

---

## Phase 5 — Sell to Salons

Salons get verified candidates, less hiring risk, faster hiring, easier filtering.

Instead of "I'm certified", they see:

- Portfolio
- Instructor score
- Employer review
- Verified badge

---

## Why Brands Would Care

Large brands (e.g. Kérastase) care about educating professionals, influencing techniques, product adoption, and loyalty.

Example: "2,800 salons are hiring colorists with balayage skills" → brand sponsors "Kérastase Professional Balayage Academy" → everyone learns using their products; brand reaches professionals before competitors.
